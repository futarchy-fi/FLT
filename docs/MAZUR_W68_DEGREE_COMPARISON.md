# W68: finite-divisor degree and component comparison

This wave completes the F1 global degree/length and component-line comparisons
listed in `MAZUR_W67_DIVISOR_LENGTH.md`. It does not prove F2–F3, L1–L2,
A7–A8, or removal of `Mazur_statement`.

## Global comparison

`IdealQuotientExact` proves that the actual map O_X → i_*O_D is an epimorphism
by its surjectivity on affine sections. Its actual kernel is the ideal module.
`DivisorIdealEvaluation` constructs the intrinsic contraction
I ⊗ O(D) → O_X and proves it is invertible on Cartier charts, then globally.
`DivisorTensorSequence` identifies the tensorized ideal inclusion with the
canonical section O_X → O(D). It uses the actual evaluation pairing and the
actual section map, rather than replacing them by arbitrary line isomorphisms.

`divisorCokernelClosedIso` from `DivisorClosedCokernel` (in namespace
`FLT.Mazur.FCurve`) identifies the actual canonical cokernel with
`(Scheme.Modules.pushforward I.subschemeι).obj
 ((Scheme.Modules.pullback I.subschemeι).obj (divisorLineBundle I hI))`.
It is a composition of global sheaf isomorphisms, using tensor exactness and
the canonical line projection formula. Chart transition compatibility is
therefore retained; no global Cartier equation is assumed.

`FiniteSchemeInvertibleSections` proves that the global section ring of a
finite scheme over a field is Artinian. Affine tensor sections turn an actual
sheaf tensor inverse into an invertible module. The semilocal Picard theorem
then constructs a rank-one free module equivalence. Thus the restricted line's
H⁰ dimension equals the structure sheaf's H⁰ dimension. Global freeness on the
finite scheme is proved, not supplied as an assumption.

`FiniteDivisorCohomology` pulls back the actual ideal/line contraction to D,
obtaining the tensor inverse required by that argument. Closed pushforward
cohomology and affine vanishing give zero positive-degree cohomology for the
actual canonical cokernel. For a proper X over a field and finite Cartier D,
`divisor_degree_eq_fieldLength` proves

```text
curveSheafDegree f (divisorLineBundle I hI) = (divisorFieldLength f I : ℤ).
```

The theorem has no H¹-vanishing or dimension-equality hypothesis. It includes
empty divisors and residue-field degrees. The cohomology and numerical
consumers use `Scheme.{0}`, as does the existing proper coherent finiteness
API. The global sheaf comparisons retain arbitrary universes.

## Components

`CartierIdealPullbackComparison` proves invertibility of the canonical ideal
pullback comparison whenever both I and its comap are Cartier. Locally, a
regular generator of the comap belongs to the ideal generated by the pulled
back equation, forcing that equation to be regular. Duality supplies the
actual divisor-line isomorphism and its canonical-section identity. Flatness
of the morphism is not required.

`FiniteDivisorComponentAvoidance` proves that a finite scheme over a field
has topological dimension at most zero. Therefore finite divisor support
cannot contain a closed subset of dimension one. On a pure one-dimensional
scheme, the earlier generic-avoidance theorem makes the restriction to every
reduced irreducible component Cartier.

`DivisorComponentDegree` combines those results. On a proper pure
one-dimensional scheme with finite effective Cartier divisor,
`divisor_component_degree_eq_length` identifies the degree of the actual
pulled-back O(D) with `reducedComponentDivisorLength`. Its positivity is
exactly intersection with support. `divisor_all_component_degrees_pos_iff`
expresses the equivalence simultaneously for every actual component.
No component-avoidance or ideal-pullback-isomorphism hypothesis remains.

## Next mathematical boundary

F2 is still unproved. Stacks 0B5Y reduces to the integral-curve ample-degree
theorem 0B5X and finite-surjective descent of ampleness 0B5V. Their source
proofs do not follow just from the F1 comparisons above. In particular,
0B5X needs degree additivity under tensor powers for arbitrary invertible
sheaves, section growth, and eventual H¹ vanishing for ideal twists. F1
computes effective-divisor degree; it does not supply those general theorems.
The existing `ProperAmpleConverse` already compares section ampleness with
closed projective power presentations on proper schemes, but does not infer
ampleness from positive degree.

The next bounded implementation target is the Euler-characteristic tensor
power formula on an integral proper curve, using actual scalar cohomology;
split its rational-section/divisor and torsion-length prerequisites into
modules of at most 240 lines. Then prove the integral criterion and finite
surjective descent before claiming 0B5Y or the smooth/polygon consequences.

L1–L2 still need arbitrary-base finite-type Z-model approximation and
fiber-to-neighborhood ampleness (0D2S). A Noetherian-only theorem is insufficient.
A7 and A8 remain after those obligations, in the order from the brief.
