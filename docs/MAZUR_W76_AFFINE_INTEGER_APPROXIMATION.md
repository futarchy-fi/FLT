# W76: affine integer approximation

This increment supplies affine pieces of L1 / Stacks 0D2S. It does not prove
global approximation, the ample-neighborhood theorem, or Mazur's theorem.

## Proved results

All declarations below are in `FLT.Mazur.Approximation`.

| Module | Main result | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `FinitePresentationIntegerModel` | `exists_common_integer_models` | A finite family of finitely presented algebras has models over a common finite-type integer subalgebra. |
| `AffineIntegerModel` | `exists_common_affine_integer_models` | The associated spectra form actual cartesian squares, with affine, locally finitely presented models. |
| `PolynomialRelationIntegerModel` | `exists_integer_model_relations` | Finitely many vanishing polynomials descend with witnesses of membership in the relation ideal. |
| `MarkedIntegerModel` | `exists_integer_model_marked` | Finitely many elements and integer polynomial equations descend to a model. |
| `HomIntegerModel` | `exists_integer_model_hom` | A map from a fixed finite-type integer algebra descends together with a model of its target. |
| `UnitIntegerModel` | `exists_integer_model_units` | Finitely many units and identities `uᵢ uⱼ = uₖ` descend, with inverses constructed in the model. |

The coefficient ring may be required to contain any specified finite set
of elements of the original base. The tensor-product equivalence recovers
the original algebra, including its algebra structure over the original
base. No injection of the model into the original algebra is assumed.

## Construction

Mathlib's `Algebra.Presentation.ModelOfHasCoeffs` supplies the quotient
model and `tensorModelOfHasCoeffsEquiv` supplies its base-change equivalence.
Taking the integer subalgebra generated by the union of finitely many
coefficient sets places all affine charts over one finite-type base.
Applying `Spec` to the tensor-product pushout produces the pullback square.

For a polynomial that vanishes in the original algebra, its coefficients
alone do not ensure vanishing in a model. Express it as a finite sum of
multiples of the presentation relations and include the coefficients of
those multipliers. Injectivity of the map of *polynomial coefficient rings*
then proves the same identity in the smaller polynomial ring. Passing to
the quotient gives the required vanishing.

Marked elements are lifted using polynomial representatives. The same
witness argument descends their equations. Maps descend by applying this
to the source's finite presentation over the integers; units descend by
including inverse equations. The unit theorem treats identities in a
single algebra, not a diagram of overlap rings.

## Remaining obligations, in order

1. Assemble a finite diagram of affine charts and overlaps over a common
   finite stage, with compatible maps, open immersions, cover conditions,
   and cocycle equalities. The existing common-model theorem only handles
   objects. The map theorem has a fixed source and does not construct a
   simultaneous diagram or prove scalar compatibility after enlargement.
2. Glue those charts and transport the local unit data through the overlap
   maps to construct the invertible sheaf and its pullback identification.
   `exists_integer_model_units` supplies equations in one ring only.
3. Descend properness and the required fiber data. The global finitely
   presented target remains the contract in `W75_L1_CONTRACT.lean`.
   The proper-only version requires proper approximation by an inverse
   system; finite presentation cannot be silently imposed.
4. Descend the actual ample fiber presentation to a finite stage.
5. Prove L2 / 0D2N over a Noetherian base, then transfer by approximation.
6. Prove A7–A8's compatible level isomorphisms and the rational-point bridge.

No later obligation is claimed from these affine results. The existing
base-neighborhood locality API still requires the ample neighborhoods as
input. The Mazur assumption has not been removed from the FLT endpoint.
